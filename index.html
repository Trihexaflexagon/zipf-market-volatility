Methodology

Data. The example pairs Reddit world-news headlines from 2008–2016 with DJIA adjusted closing prices (J. Sun, 2016). This is not ticker-specific news or the FNSPID dataset.

Zipf score. Words are counted and ranked each day. The negative slope of log(word frequency) against log(rank) is Zipf α. We compare that value with the median α of the previous N news days. Changes in text length are measured separately as a baseline.

Dates. Headlines only have dates, not reliable publication times. News dated D is compared with the return from the D+1 close to the D+2 close. The date controls limit which observations enter the study.

Test. Early observations set the thresholds. Two observations are removed at the split, and AUC and backtest returns use only the later holdout. High Zipf scores move the test strategy into cash; transaction costs are included.

Limits. Headline count, length and timing can affect the signal. Repeatedly changing settings risks overfitting. A correlation here does not establish prediction or causation.
